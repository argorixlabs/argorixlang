\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

An agentic application is not merely a model invocation.  It is a composition
of principals, messages, provider operations, tools, policy decisions, and
evidence.  When these concerns are distributed among prompts, deployment
configuration, and application code, a reviewer cannot determine from one
artifact which operation was authorized, which boundary was enforced, or which
claim is only aspirational.  Protocols such as MCP and A2A standardize useful
interaction surfaces \citep{mcp_spec_2025,a2a_spec}, while identity and
credential standards define interoperable data models
\citep{w3c_did_core_2022,w3c_vc_data_model_2025}.  They do not by themselves
make an application policy executable or its local execution evidence
reproducible.

\Argorix addresses this systems boundary by compiling declarations for agents,
messages, passports, policies, providers, governance, and evidence into a
validated intermediate representation and bytecode executed by a constrained
virtual machine \citep{argorixlang2026}.  The core design decision is to retain
source-faithful declarations until semantic validation, then lower only
validated constructs.  Runtime decisions produce a trace, a SecurityReport,
and an EvidenceBundle containing semantic SHA-256 digests.  An executable
provider registry is separate from declarative external-provider contracts.
In the studied configuration, only \code{simulated} is executable.

The paper asks five explicit research questions:
\begin{description}
\item[RQ1.] How can governance, sovereign identity, and evidence requirements
be represented as compiled language constructs?
\item[RQ2.] How does the \Argorix pipeline constrain a chatbot request before a
provider operation can be planned?
\item[RQ3.] What evidence does the runtime generate, and how can its internal
consistency be checked offline?
\item[RQ4.] What do the existing runtime sessions demonstrate, and what do they
not demonstrate?
\item[RQ5.] How can Agent Passports, ATrust, DCP-AI, and future sovereign
discovery fit into an open agentic-web architecture?
\end{description}

Our contributions are: (1) a language architecture that exposes governance and
sovereign metadata to compilation; (2) a fail-closed runtime/provider boundary
with explicit negative controls; (3) an evidence model separating artifact
integrity from policy judgment; (4) a reproducible observational study that
reports complete, incomplete, and internally inconsistent outcomes; and (5) a
claim-bounded roadmap connecting implemented passport identifiers to proposed
sovereign discovery.
